\section{Conclusions}

This study developed Agent-MD as an architecture that combines a reasoning agent for flexible planning and event-triggered review, a rule-based campaign agent for persistent production, scientific computation and analysis tools, and explicit campaign state and provenance records. Once the researcher approves the campaign specification, the rule-based campaign agent selects and dispatches actions for system construction, simulation, analysis, continuation, archiving, provenance validation, and progression between simulation states. The reasoning agent remains available for review events that cannot be safely resolved through the approved rules.

Agent-MD was demonstrated through a montmorillonite GCMC--MD desorption campaign comprising five molecular systems and three RH states. The high-level scientific request was converted into a compact model matrix and 15 provenance-linked simulation states, all of which reached strict-pass archived status. Sampling requirements varied substantially among states, confirming the value of state-specific continuation rather than a uniform production length. The completed campaign involved 414 campaign-agent operations and 120 run--analyze--decide cycles, while only one production state required review and no automated reasoning-agent invocation occurred during formal production. Analysis of the K-LC0.4 RH = 0.3 incident showed that the critical issue was not corruption of the simulation counter, but an incorrect mapping between inherited absolute timestep and RH-local sampling progress. Correcting this state-level interpretation allowed the campaign to resume and complete. Frozen replay of this incident and a stale-artifact provenance conflict further showed how structured workflow evidence could support targeted and conservative review.

These results indicate that effective agentic control does not require an LLM to remain inside every step of a scientific workflow. Routine autonomy can be provided by a rule-based campaign agent operating over approved rules and explicit workflow state, while flexible reasoning is reserved for decisions that cannot be safely resolved by those rules. More broadly, the development of agent-enabled science will require scientific workflows to become increasingly machine-readable, restartable, provenance-aware, and explicit about when rule-based production pauses, when review is requested, and how validated actions return to the production agent. Agent-MD provides one implementation of this principle for GCMC--MD campaigns, while further validation across additional simulation methods, production incidents, and reasoning agents will be required to establish its wider applicability.
